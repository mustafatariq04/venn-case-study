Here's a high-level overview of how Venn makes money: it earns when money sits in it, moves through it, or is spent on its cards.

Why it matters: FX is Venn's moat against the banks and its largest lever for cross-border customers. Anything that keeps those customers active compounds it.

Interchange is about 2.0% on commercial Mastercards (TD rate chart). The issuer (Peoples Trust) and card processors keep part of it; their cut isn't public, so 0.5% is my assumption. Cashback is paid from what Venn keeps.

Key insight · Business model

Venn makes more money when more of a customer's money sits in, moves through, or is spent on Venn.

More balances

Customers keep more of their cash in Venn, especially in USD, which earns Venn the larger margin.

More money movement

More FX conversions and transfers run through Venn, at rates customers can't get from their bank.

More card spend

More company spend goes on Venn cards, which today is capped by the cash customers keep in Venn.

SoThe next product should be judged by how much it grows these three.

1.4Goals, hypotheses and criteria

The two company goals pull in different directions, and the best bet serves both: more active businesses widens the base, and higher ARPU deepens each relationship.

What Venn optimizes for

Active businesses
At least one revenue-generating transaction in a month. A sign-up that never funds or spends doesn't count.
ARPU
Grown by deeper product adoption: more balances, spend and payment flows per customer.
ICP
Canadian-incorporated, 2–100 employees, outside Quebec, which needs its own provincial licence.
Long-term strategy
The default banking and finance platform for Canadian businesses, 10x better and 10x cheaper than the status quo.

Hypotheses to validate

The questions I'd answer with Venn's internal data before committing to a bet.

  1. H1
    The prepaid ceiling is costing card spend.

    Every purchase must be covered by cash already in Venn. Customers who keep most of their cash elsewhere, or whose big bills land before revenue, get declined or move the spend to another card.

    How I'd test it

    Card spend relative to balance, top-ups before large bills, and declines for insufficient funds.

    Informs

    Credit cards
  2. H2
    Many customers are FX-first, not primary-account users.

    They come for cheap FX and keep their main account at a bank.

    How I'd test it

    Share of active businesses whose payroll, rent or tax payments leave from Venn.

    Informs

    Payroll
  3. H3
    "Active" and "signed up" differ meaningfully.

    The 15,000+ figure likely counts sign-ups, not monthly actives.

    How I'd test it

    Monthly active rate by sign-up cohort and plan.

    Informs

    All three
  4. H4
    The card program is already credit-adjacent.

    Venn's public disclosure calls it a charge card, while the case describes a pre-funded model.

    How I'd test it

    Confirm the current program and what credit would require from the issuing partner.

    Informs

    Credit cards
  5. H5
    Reimbursements are partially built.

    Today they appear only on the Custom plan.

    How I'd test it

    Demand from lower plans, and how much of the workflow already exists.

    Informs

    Reimbursements

2.3Customer demand: the survey and the market data

Venn's survey tells us what customers ask for; national data tells us how much they need it. I'd read both before weighting the requests.

Venn's customer survey: read it critically

  • Who asked. Requests from high-balance, high-spend customers count for more than requests from dormant accounts.
  • Who didn't answer. Businesses that left, or never activated, because cards were pre-funded aren't in a customer survey, so it under-counts them.
  • Why they asked. "Credit card" might mean rewards, working capital, or simply not pre-funding. Each points to a different product.
  • What they use today. If customers already run payroll elsewhere happily, switching costs are high even if they say they want it.

Market data on credit · Canadian SMEs, 2023

  • 49% of SMEs requested external financing, close to the pre-pandemic 47%.
  • Of those that didn't, 81% said they didn't need it.
  • Business credit cards cost 19% interest on average, against 11% for lines of credit.
  • Approval rates were high, 90.9% for debt financing, and rise with firm size.

Key insight · Demand

Most small businesses want the convenience of a credit card more than the borrowing.

SoA credit card paid in full each month (spend now, settle from the Venn account later, no revolving interest) fits that need and fits Venn's balance-driven model.

Source: ISED, Survey on Financing and Growth of SMEs 2023

3.1Credit cards

High value, and now expected. Float, Loop and Ramp already offer credit, and it removes the cap on card spend, so it drives both customer growth and revenue.

What customers are asking for

The ability to spend on a Venn card without pre-funding it: pay for ads, inventory and travel now, and settle later. For most small businesses the appeal is convenience, not borrowing: 81% of SMEs that didn't seek financing in 2023 said they didn't need it (2.3).

Serves most: Cross-border founders Finance-led scale-ups

Value: customer growth

  • Wins businesses that need card credit and today choose Float, Loop, Ramp or a bank card instead.
  • Reduces churn risk among existing customers as rivals court them, especially after Ramp's July 2026 launch.
  • Every card purchase is a revenue-generating transaction, so higher spend keeps accounts "active".

Value: revenue (ARPU)

  • Removes the ceiling on interchange, the stream that grows with a customer's business (Section 1.2).
  • Statements are paid from the Venn account, so customers keep more cash with Venn, and USD balances earn a real margin (1.2).
  • Margin depends on the plan: uncapped Pro cashback absorbs most of it, so rewards need care.

Money flow it brings · example 20-person firm

+$240K

card spend a year moved onto Venn, for a firm that keeps $30K in Venn but wants to put $50K a month on cards.

About +$3,600 a year in extra interchange on Plus, after issuer costs. Net of funding costs and losses, about +$2,300.

Link to long-term strategy

Be the default banking and finance platform for Canadian businesses (1.4).
The gap today
Customers can only spend what they've already deposited, so their bigger purchases go on another company's card.
How it helps
All of a business's card spending can run through Venn. Paid in full each month, with no interest or personal guarantee, it keeps Venn's low-cost promise.

3.2Payroll

Strong long-term value, but slow to pay off. It would make Venn the main account for more businesses, but few switch payroll mid-year, it's compliance-heavy, and Venn's direct spend rivals don't offer it either.

What customers are asking for

Run payroll where the money already is: pay employees, remit CPP, EI and income tax to CRA, and issue T4s and ROEs, without a separate tool and a transfer between accounts.

Serves most: Operating SMBs with field teams Finance-led scale-ups

Value: customer growth

  • Payroll runs on a fixed schedule, often every two weeks, so it guarantees recurring transactions and active accounts.
  • Could attract domestic SMBs that don't need cheap FX, widening Venn's appeal.
  • Switching payroll mid-year is painful, so new adoption will be slow.

Value: revenue (ARPU)

  • A fee line: Wagepoint's Unlimited plan charges $40 a month plus $6 per employee, about $1,900 a year for 20 staff.
  • Funds land in Venn before each pay run, lifting balances and making Venn the primary account.
  • Payroll itself earns little interchange or FX.

Large flow, thin direct margin. The value is primacy and stickiness more than revenue.

4.1The recommendation

My recommendation

Launch a Venn credit card for existing customers: purchases draw on their Venn cash first, and credit covers the rest up to a limit set from their Venn history, paid in full each month.

Who

Existing Venn customers whose card spend outgrows the cash they keep in Venn, starting with Plus and Pro.

What's different

No documents, no due-diligence call, no $50K minimum. Venn underwrites on balance and cash-flow data it already has.

How it earns

Interchange on spend that used to decline or go elsewhere. Venn funds only the gap, about a third of the capital a full-float credit card ties up (4.3).

Not nowFull float on all spend (Phase 2), revolving credit, payroll and credit for brand-new customers. Reimbursements ship alongside as a quick win.

06 · Summary

Build a Venn credit card for existing customers

Sections 1 to 5 point to the same answer. Of the three requests, credit cards matter most for Venn's growth and revenue, and the way to build them is a cash-first card underwritten on data Venn already has.

The recommendation

Build a cash-first Venn credit card for existing customers: purchases use their Venn cash first, the credit card covers the rest up to a limit set from their Venn history, and it's paid in full each month.

Why credit cards

Card spend is the revenue stream that grows with each customer, and a pre-funded card caps it at the cash on hand. Float, Loop and Ramp all offer credit; Venn doesn't.

Why this design

Customers stop hitting the cash ceiling without taking on debt, and Venn funds only the gap. Underwriting on Venn's own balance data keeps risk bounded and sign-up instant.

Size of the prize

About 840 customers at scale, $100M of new card spend and $1.2M of new revenue a year, for about $4.8M of capital. The beta needs about $0.5M.

HowValidate in two months, beta with a randomized holdout by month six, and scale only on proven lift and losses within budget. Reimbursements ship alongside; payroll follows through an embedded partner.

The three requests, in order

  1. Credit cards: build now. The biggest growth and revenue lever, and the gap every direct rival has filled (3.1, 3.4).
  2. Reimbursements: ship alongside. A quick win that extends a feature Venn already has (3.3).
  3. Payroll: later, with a partner. The deepest long-term moat, once more customers use Venn as their main account (3.2).

What I'd do first

  • Validate the cash ceiling with Venn's data: card spend vs balance, top-ups and declines (H1).
  • Confirm what the issuing partner needs to support credit (H4).
  • Agree the loss budget and beta size with risk and finance, then start the build.

Thank you for reading. I'd welcome the chance to pressure-test these assumptions with Venn's own data.
